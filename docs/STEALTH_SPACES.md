# DF30 — Stealth spaces: cover, hiding, and what each Y plane means

> **Status: owner round 49o, 2026-11-07. Phase 1 (hiding + the noise it is tied to) is BUILT; round 49t (2026-11-11) added hiding BEHIND a sofa / an armchair, the wall beside a
> DOORWAY, and darkness as a hiding place with a working torch (§10, and `TORCH_AND_DARK.md`). Creeping behind furniture as a walking plane (the "cover" row) is still designed, not built.**
> Owner's words: *"wire in more stealth interactions… additional Y planes for moving into environments… moving in crouch behind furniture like moving behind a sofa in order for enemies to not see the player and move past, giving the player more room. Same with hiding. When a player hides in a stealth spot, the screen should go very washed out and dark, with only the player really being visible, then the enemy passing the player which we can see, but once it moves past the enemy is washed out faded just like everything else. We can even zoom in on the player during this effect so it looks claustrophobic."* Then: *"because of this, we may need more contextual elements to the various Y planes"* and *"a prompt appear for those areas where the player can hide."*

Then: *"stealth, hiding and noise are intricately linked, so this is the point where all of that needs to work well together. For example, if a player enters a module and immediately has a hide prompt to hide against the module wall, enemies can move past; when the player leaves the hiding spot they may need to crouch and move quietly, otherwise their noise of regular movement will alert those same enemies, who will then turn around."*

This extends `SOUND_STEALTH.md` (noise) and `NPC_AI.md` (what the dead perceive). It does not change the noise radii.

## 1. What is true today (read from the code, not remembered)

- The dead see by a **radius**: `distance <= detection` (standard 100, big 130 ± 45; `enemy_zombie_standard.gd` ~1358, `enemy_zombie_big.gd` ~508). No facing, no line of sight, no crouch modifier. A loud noise or an `alert_timer` raises it to 2000 (omniscient) unless the noise carried a source (round 39: then it walks to the source, looks about for 4 s and gives up — `EnemyMind`).
- Crouch only shrinks the **noise** you make (radius 45 while moving). It does not make you harder to *see*.
- Planes that exist: the **lane** (feet 353 in a flat, 419 in a corridor), the **back plane** (feet 339, `back_plane_spot.gd`: step up to search set-back furniture; enemies still see and reach you), the **balcony** (feet 319, its own enemy plane, `enemy_plane.gd`).
- Hiding places exist as art but not as a mechanic: planned wardrobes / cupboards open when searched; every bed has the `underbed` gap.
- The lift, doors, bodies, drops… all answer `[interact]` through `InteractArbiter` (round 49n): the nearest wins.

## 2. The plane context table

The owner's point: a plane is not just a Y value. Each one needs the same set of answers, kept in ONE place so a new plane cannot forget one. (`scripts/stealth.gd` `PLANES` is the single source; this table is its prose.)

| Plane | Where | Feet | How you get there | Do the dead see you? | Can they reach you? | Your noise | Screen | Prompt |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lane** | everywhere | 353 / 419 | default | by radius, as today | yes | by gait | normal | doors, lifts, bodies (arbiter) |
| **Back plane** | set-back furniture with a live node | 339 | `[W]` in scavenge mode | yes | yes (a step back, not a hiding place) | scavenge | normal | tutorial arrow only |
| **Balcony** | study / dining balcony | 319 | `[W]` | only the dead on the balcony | only the dead on it | — | normal | standing pill (round 49c) |
| **Cover** *(phase 2)* | behind a low piece ≥ 30 px tall (sofa, bed, table, sideboard, chest, desk) | ~335 (lane − 18, behind the piece's base) | crouch + `[W]` at the piece | **no** — the piece blocks sight; revealed only inside `COVER_REVEAL` (24 px) of the dead, or if you stand / sprint / strike | they walk past on the lane; if one finds you it is on top of you | crouch (45 px) | slight desaturation, nothing else | **"Duck behind the sofa  [W]"** |
| **Hidden** *(phase 1; 49t)* | in or behind a piece a person would really use: a wardrobe (bedroom), a bath or a shower behind its curtain (bathroom), **behind a sofa / loveseat / armchair**, **standing flat against the wall beside a doorway**, **in the dark** (night, lights out, torch off); later under a bed | on / in / behind the piece | ONE `[interact]` press at the piece, any stance | **no**, at all — but see §4 *too close* and *finding* | no, until found | none (still) | **real lighting brought right down: a very dull cool ambient, the player's own small pool, colours kept, no outline — and a slow 1.1× push-in** (§5) | **"Wardrobe  [E] Hide" / "Bath  [E] Get in" / "Shower  [E] Hide behind the curtain"**, then "[E] Come out" — all hung right over the head |

Contextual elements every plane must state (the checklist for the next plane someone adds): *entry verb · exit verb · what blocks sight · what the dead can still do · noise · camera · prompt text · draw order against the furniture · what ends it (a hit, a noise, a scene change, death) · how a save treats it (always on the lane — `lane_position()`).*

## 3. The prompt (owner: "a prompt appear for those areas where the player can hide"; round 49q: "automatic, not a move up or down… just a hide prompt… and then another press and the player leaves")

Hiding is **one press of `[interact]`, in any stance** — no up key, no crouch to learn (the player is put down in there). A **standing world pill** (the same `HUD.show_world_prompt` the balcony uses) appears whenever a hiding place is in reach and the player is not already in it:

- `Wardrobe   [E] Hide` · `Bath   [E] Get in` · `Shower   [E] Hide behind the curtain` — standing or crouched, the same
- inside: `[E] Come out`
- the press goes through the **InteractArbiter** like every other `[interact]`: the nearest answerer takes it (a door right beside the wardrobe gets it, not the wardrobe), and in scavenge stance a live search at the same piece owns it

**Where text hangs (owner round 49q: *"the text for come out is just too damn high. We really need to stop having text so high"*):** the hide pills hang **right over the player's head** (`StealthSpot._over_head`, `Player.head_top_position`), not up the wall at the piece's top; and the HUD now clamps **every** world pill: its anchor may stand no more than `HUD.PROMPT_MAX_RISE` (12 world px) above the top of the player's head (`_prompt_ceiling_y`), so a lift's, a workbench's or a curtain rail's own label point can no longer put a pill a screen's height up (a prompt for something at the player's feet is left where it is; with no player nothing is clamped). The player's speech bubbles sit on the head with a tail, and the pills step round them (`hud_prompt_test`).

**Hiding must make sense (owner, on a screenshot of a player "hiding" beside a toilet: *"hiding beside a toilet is unnatural — it would make more sense to jump into the bathtub and hide in there"*, then *"hiding must contextually make sense"*).** There is no generic "against the wall" spot: a bare wall is nowhere to hide, and the wall nook by the front door that the first build had is gone. A place to hide is a **piece a person would hide in, in the room it stands in**, and that is DATA with a check that can fail: `Stealth.HOW` states each way of hiding — the only rooms it can happen in and the least a piece must measure (a person has to fit): *inside* a piece = a bedroom's wardrobe (≥ 34 × 60), *in* a piece = a bathroom's bath (≥ 90 × 40). `Stealth.HIDE_PIECES` lists the pieces (wardrobe_wood / _mirror / _small, bath), and `Stealth.hide_rule_problems()` re-checks that table against the furniture library — a toilet, a basin, a sofa, a trunk, a linen cupboard, a wardrobe "in a bath" or a bath "inside" are each refused by name in `stealth_test` (the rule is shown to fail on them). Not hiding places, on purpose: a linen cupboard / larder (a person does not fit), a sofa or table (that is *cover*, phase 2), a walk-in shower (glass: you would be seen), a bed (needs the dark gap drawn first).

**The bath** (`StealthSpot.add_for` reads three numbers per drawn variant, measured off the sprites): the player climbs in (they rise `stand_up` 6 px above the tub's feet line, onto its floor) and the **front of the tub is drawn over them** — a copy of the piece's rows from the front lip down, laid over the player on the actors' layer (z 1, above the player, below every dead thing added to the flat after) so the lip hides their shins and their head and shoulders show over the rim. `lip_up` is how many rows above the feet line the lip begins: roll-top 18, gold 17, avocado 21, wetroom 23, pink 22 (`lip_rows(def, file)`; a variant it does not know gets 20). **The shower-bath with its curtain drawn** (`bath_curtain`, the wetroom's) is hidden **behind the curtain** (owner: *"the showers too… hide behind those, slicing behind the curtains like with stairwells"*): the WHOLE curtain sprite is the cover, laid over the player at 90 % so a dark figure shows through the plastic, and it fades in over the step in (and out as they step back), so the player is sliced by the curtain as they go behind it instead of popping; the place is called a *shower* and the verb is "Hide behind the curtain". The pink bath's curtain is pulled back — it hides nobody, so that tub hides by its lip like the others.

Keys are written as `{action}` tokens (never a literal key), so the pad / touch prompts follow (`docs/CONTROLS.md`). Where a hide spot and a search node share a wardrobe, **search wins in scavenge stance while the node is still live** (`StealthSpot.search_owns_the_key`: the arbiter's nearest-answerer rule); hiding is for combat stance or a searched-out piece. If the owner wants hiding to win, the dial is `StealthSpot.yield_to_search`.

## 4. The rules (phase 1: hiding)

1. **Enter** (`[interact]` at a hide spot, any stance): it always works — but a dead thing **too close** when you commit has you. **Too close = within its own attack reach and one more length** (`Stealth.too_close` / `zone_of`: `TOO_CLOSE_LENGTHS` 2 x its `_attack_reach()`, capped at `TOO_CLOSE_MAX` 160 px so a spitter's 300 px spit is not a "reach"; a standard on the deep floors 76 px, a big 126): it saw you go in, and after a short beat (`DISCOVER_DELAY` 0.35 s — enough to hear the breath and feel the dark) it has found you. **No second chance** (owner: *"it's on the player if they are caught… they could have gone further into the apartment"*): nothing brings you out of that beat (`Player._hide_doomed_by`), then `found_hiding` throws you out and it is on you (`"It's found me."`). A knocked-down or lying dead thing does not count. Outside the zone, hiding works — **even while they are chasing you**.
2. **While hidden** the dead cannot *see* you at all: `Stealth.sight_blocked` turns their detection to −1 and their reach to INF in the same two places the radius lives (standard + big; spitter / crawler / long-arm inherit). A chaser (or a follower locked on with no source, `alert_timer`) that loses you **shambles past** (owner: *"the enemy will shamble past… that could result in the player now being stuck with enemies between them and where they were"*): `Stealth.pass_by` sends it on the way it was going, **through** the place you hide (rule 3) and `PASS_BEYOND` 240 px out the far side, where it stops and looks about for 4 s — it does **not** go and stand at the place it last saw you (that is your hiding place: it would find you). It ends on the far side of you: the dead are between you and where you were heading. The lock-on ENDS (`lose_track`), so it cannot charge across the flat the moment you come out.
3. **The dead walk through you.** While you are hidden the player's body lets the dead within 56 px pass through (`Player._hide_phase`: the same mutual collision exception the anti-stuck net uses, released by it once they are clear). Without it a dead thing walking past would stand pressed against the wardrobe, blind, for ever. "Enemies can move past" is literal.
4. **Finding** (so a hiding place is a risk, not a wall, even when you dived in clear): a dead thing that is *searching* (it has arrived at a noise or at your last-seen spot) within `FIND_RANGE` (70 px) of you for `FIND_TIME` (2.5 s) **finds** you: you are thrown out of the hiding place, it is on you (`EnemyMind.spotted`). Walking *past* does not find you. So the skill is: break sight first, hide *away* from where they last saw you. (Consequence worth knowing: a follower that walks in locked on to you loses you, searches where it stands and, if that is within 70 px of your hiding place, finds you — hide before they are close, or in a place further from the door than that.)
5. **Noise — one system, not two.** Hiding adds no new noise numbers; it plugs into the existing ones (`SOUND_STEALTH.md`):
   - **Hidden = silent.** You are not moving, so the gait noise is 0; the dead beside you are not alerted.
   - **Coming out is a footstep, not a reset.** The way out keeps your stance: `[interact]` (or a click to walk) leaves you **crouched**, so a creeping step carries `crouch` 45 px; standing and walking carries `walk` 120 px, running 240 px. A dead thing that has just passed — say 110 px off, beyond its sight (100) but inside a walk's reach — **does not hear a crouched step and does hear a standing one, and turns round for it** (`EnemyMind.hear`: it walks to the noise, looks about, and chases only what it then sees). The game says so once, when it matters: *"They're close. Stay low."* if any of the dead are within a walking step's reach as you come out.
   - **A piece is not silent to leave**: each has its own noise of coming out (`HIDE_PIECES[...].noise`, 0.4 s) — a wardrobe's door 40 px, water slopping off a bath 55 px (the bath is the louder way out; `stealth_test` pins that).
   - **Sight is still a full circle.** The dead have no facing today (`distance <= detection`), so a dead thing that has just passed *within 100 px* sees you the moment you are out. "Passing behind" only works from further than that. A **sight cone** (they see ahead, not behind) is the obvious next step and would make this whole system richer; it changes the dead's AI and every fight, so it is the owner's call and not built.
   - **Light** (night makes a hiding place deeper) is the other dial noise has not met yet; designed in §6, not built.
6. **Leave**: **one more `[interact]` press**, or a click to walk elsewhere (which walks you out). Nothing else does — the walking keys, attack, shove (`push`), sprint and the crouch toggle all do **nothing** in there (a hidden player cannot swing or shove from the wardrobe: `_handle_attack_press` swallows a key press, `_do_push` is not reached, a buffered swing waits). A hit or any damage throws you out. Anything else that takes over the body (a door's approach walk, a lash, a listen, the pack, a scripted scene) ends it, and while you are hidden **nothing else answers `[interact]`** (`InteractArbiter`) so the press that brings you out cannot also open the door beside the wardrobe. Death, a scene change, a freed player: the state, the camera, the room's light and the player's own light all go back **exactly** (tested). `Player.HIDE_GRACE_MSEC` 250 stops the press that hid you from also bringing you out.
7. **Survivors** (friendly) are unaffected; hostile residents stay as they are.

## 5. The dark (the owner's description, made precise — SIX passes)

`scripts/stealth_fx.gd` — a CanvasLayer on layer 0 (above the world, *below* the HUD at layer 1, so the HUD and the prompt stay readable). History on the owner's screenshots: a dark vignette → a black circular hole → TV static with spotlights and a zoom → a flat pale grey fog ("I really don't like it… the fog effect and the greying out") → the Last-of-Us listen-mode look made by post-processing the picture (a luminance map, the player redrawn as a copy, the dead as black shapes with a glowing outline; *"no pulsating"*) — on which the owner said: ***"reduce the lighting considerably to a very dull low light. We want tension here, not this dull lifeless look. Dynamic real lighting is so important because so much of our colour palette looks so lifeless… We need lighting, shadows, it makes such a huge difference to the tension and helps with the heavy lifting for these colours. When that works well, you don't even need the outline around the enemy, because the natural colours of the enemy will be stronger and more striking."***

So it is **lighting now, not a filter**:

- **The room's ambient light** (the world's `WorldGrade` CanvasModulate) is brought to a very dull, cool, low level (`HIDE_AMBIENT` 0.07 luminance, `AMBIENT_TINT`) — never brighter than it already was (`dark_ambient`), so the night run (already ~0.03) hardly changes. It is **borrowed and given back exactly**: if something re-grades the room while you hide (a floor going live) that becomes the colour to return to; a freed player or a scene change gives it back (`_exit_tree`).
- **The lights themselves** are most of what a morning room is lit by (an additive window, a beam, the aura), so the picture is also dimmed in proportion (`gain_for`: the square root of how far the ambient fell — a morning room to about 0.27, an afternoon one 0.41, a night one not at all), **per colour channel with a touch of gamma** (`POST_GAMMA` 1.25) so a lit pool stays brighter than the dark round it and **every colour stays its own** (the green tile, the pink bath, a dead thing's skin) — and a vignette (`VIGNETTE_EDGE` 0.16 at the screen's edge, `VIGNETTE_CENTRE` 1.4 round the player) that closes in. That is the whole post-process; nothing in it moves (`stealth_test` fails on `TIME`, `sin(`, `hash(`, `fog`, a luminance `dot(`).
- **The player's own light** (`PlayerAura`, the PointLight2D every player carries) is raised to a small dim pool while they hide (`GLOW_ENERGY` 0.8, `GLOW_SCALE` 1.5 ≈ 190 px) — never dimmer than their own; and given back. **The player and the dead are lit by it like everything else, in their own colours: nothing is copied over the picture, nothing is outlined** (the copies, the outline shader and the black body are DELETED; `stealth_test` checks the constants are gone). Beyond the pool it is simply dark.
- **A sound carries the tension** (`tools/gen_hide_audio.py` → `assets/audio/stealth/`): a slow deep breath as they slip in (`breath_in.wav`), then a low heartbeat (`heartbeat_deep.wav`, ~54 bpm, nothing above ~500 Hz) that goes on while they are still — a touch quicker and louder the nearer the dead are (`Stealth.focus_for_gap`, pitch ×1.22, +4 dB) — and a breath let go (`breath_out.wav`) as they come out. **The heart is only a sound: no pulsing anything on screen.**
- **A gentle push-in** (`ZOOM_IN` 1.10, smoothstep with the intensity) with the player held exactly where they stand on the screen (the centre moves toward them), put back exactly (and not undone if something else has re-fitted the camera since).

Rendered under xvfb (flat 1703's tiled bath, a standard dead thing 90 px off): the room dim, cool and dull with its colours kept, the dead thing a dark-green figure standing on the normal floor line, the pill right over the head. **Seen as stills only.** **Not seen:** a dead thing walking through the pool in motion; the night run's look (the capture environment showed the night room fully bright before the hide, maybe a lightning frame — not chased); a real monitor. The numbers (`HIDE_AMBIENT`, `GLOW_*`, `VIGNETTE_*`, `POST_*`) are first guesses for the owner's eye.

**A mistake worth recording (owner: *"why on earth is the enemy on that upper Y plane?"*):** the first screenshot of the grey screen showed the dead thing standing high up the wall. That was `tools/scene_capture.tscn`'s new `zombie:<x>` step, which put it at the player's own y — and a hidden player stands RAISED in the tub. The game was right (the dead stand on the one floor line; the screen copies them where they are); the tool now spawns on `lane_position()`. Nothing in the game moved a dead thing.

All of it is a pure function of `intensity` (0..1), so a pause, a death or a scene change can only ever leave it at a value a test can read.

## 6. Cover — phase 2 (designed, not built)

A **second Y line behind a low piece**: `[W]` crouched at a cover-eligible piece (data: `kind` in sofa / loveseat / bed / dining table / kitchen table / sideboard / chest of drawers / desk / low bookcase / trunk, and `h ≥ 30`, i.e. taller than the crouched player) steps the player up onto the line ~18 px behind the piece's base, **crouched, drawn behind the piece** (that piece's sprite z-sorts above the actors while the player is in its cover — the one rule the draw order needs). Along the piece's own width you can **creep** (crouch speed, noise 45 → 0 at half pace); standing, sprinting, striking or leaving its ends drops you to the lane. The dead on the lane do not see you unless within `COVER_REVEAL`; they walk past — *"more room"*: a dead thing investigating a noise walks the full lane through the room instead of stopping at you, which is what makes the sofa worth reaching. Open questions for the owner: whether cover also blocks *noise* (I propose it halves the radius), and whether the dead may *investigate* a cover spot they pass twice (I propose no in v1).

**Corridors** get the same on their dressing (hall table, planter, pram, bin bags, shoe rack): phase 3, after the flat version has been felt. **Light** is the contextual dial both want: on the night run a spot outside a lamp pool is harder to see into (`sight × 0.5`); morning is `× 1.0`.

## 8. Why hide when you can push? (owner round 49q — "the final piece of the gameplay loop puzzle")

A shove used to cost 28 of 100 and could be thrown again and again, so any crowd could be shoved through and hiding was never the better choice. The shove now **costs a bar and then a pause, on every floor from the first** (owner round 49r: *"it needs to be baked in from go otherwise players will feel a bait and switch for no reason, like they are being nerfed in real time"* — round 49q had put it on the descent ramp, floor 25 down; that was my reading of the round-49n instruction, not the owner's, and it is gone: no `descent_ramp` anywhere in the shove, floor 30 and the tutorial included):

- **A shove needs a FULL bar and wipes it** (`WorldState.push_cost()`: the whole bar divided by the character's `get_push_charges()`; `push_min_stamina()`: a hair under the cost, so a bar that has refilled to 99 % still says *"I need my wind back to shove."*). A bar below 10 says *"Too exhausted to push."*
- **Two charges**: **Joe** (blond_man, the Tenant: "Two shoves from a full bar (everyone else gets one)") has two, each half a bar; the **Brawler** perk (`B_brawler`: "One more shove from a full bar, and shove 50% harder") adds one to anyone (they add: Joe with it has three). Everyone else, from base, has one. (The old `push_cost` multipliers on Joe 0.70 and the Brawler 0.60, and `get_push_cost_mult`, are gone: the cost is the bar over the charges, so a multiplier would have promised something the game no longer delivers.)
- **The bar does not start to come back at once** (`WorldState.PUSH_RECOVERY` 2.5 s of no regeneration after a shove; was 0.8 s). The old repeat-doubling of the cost (a second shove within 0.6 s cost double) is gone: the bar itself is the limit.
- Locked by `stamina_test._test_push_every_floor` (floors 30 / 29 / 28 / 27 / 26 / 25 / 20 / 5 / 1 with the ramp forced to -1, 0 and 1: a full bar goes in one shove, 99 % is refused, the same wait) and `_test_push_charges` (Joe's two on floor 30).

So: one shove buys one body out of the way and leaves you winded for a long beat with the dead still coming — and **hiding becomes the other answer**, with its own risks (too close / finding / the dead between you and where you were going). Numbers are first guesses: `WorldState.PUSH_RECOVERY`, the charges.

## 9. Light and stealth — the next thread (owner: "stealth and lighting need to go hand in hand… too colourful in certain areas, and too well lit… even if that means hiding in darkness"; then "dynamic real lighting is so important because so much of our colour palette looks so lifeless")

**Round 49s built the first part of this — see `docs/LIGHT_AND_DEPTH.md`:** rooms and corridors now keep shade where light does not reach, furniture and props cast shadows away from the light, a flat's window light pools round the glass at dusk and by night, and every actor has a contact shadow. Still NOT built: darkness as a place to hide, real-time shadows of bodies, light as a stealth dial. The notes below are the facts that pass started from:

- **What the lighting is today** (CLAUDE.md "REAL 2D lighting", measured in this round): a per-scene ambient `WorldGrade` — morning 0.95 (near-fully lit, "lighting barely intrudes"), afternoon 0.42, night 0.07, each dimmed a little by depth — plus real PointLight2Ds: wall sconces (corridors), apartment lamps by seed, window lights + beams, fire, the player's aura (0.10 / 0.40 / 0.62 by run). A probe of a run-3 flat found the wall at 0.20 brightness with the ambient at 0.03 and **0.18 of it from lights** — the lights, not the ambient, decide how a room looks even at night; the morning room is bright almost regardless of any light.
- **The owner's observation:** many rooms read as one image — uniform walls / floors / furniture colour, too evenly lit. Per-piece depth and weight (the furniture redraws) is half the answer; the other half is **light and shadow carrying the colours**: pools and falloff, dark corners, a lit window's shaft, shadows cast by furniture (no light casts shadows today — `PointLight2D.shadow_enabled` is off everywhere, no occluders exist).
- **Stealth wants it:** a hiding place in darkness should be deeper than one in a lamp's pool (the `sight x 0.5` outside a pool idea in §6); the dead should be lit by what lights them, in their own colours (the hide screen already relies on that); a sconce cutting out is a stealth event as much as an atmosphere one. The dials to turn first: a darker day (the morning ambient and the beam / window energies), shadows from a few key lights, an occluder on furniture and doors, `Stealth` reading the light at the player's spot. **Do not change the global lighting without the owner's eye** — this round only moved the lights while the player hides, and gave everything back exactly.

## 10. Round 49t — hiding behind furniture, beside a doorway, and in the dark

The owner: *"we need to ensure stealth hiding spots are clear and present whenever they contextually should be there. So jumping behind a sofa, hiding behind or beside a
chair, hiding up against a module wall for enemies to pass by, hiding behind a shower curtain or in a bathtub or in a wardrobe. When the furniture has physical real shape, it
means that we can specifically identify a place where a player can actively hide within or beside or behind."*

**Behind a piece** (`Stealth.HOW["behind"]`, pieces `sofa`, `loveseat`, `armchair`; planned rooms): `Sofa   [E] Hide behind it`. The player crouches on the wall side of the
piece and **the piece is drawn over them, with everything that stands in front of it** — the coffee table across a sofa's front, what is on that table — copied over it in the
room's own order (`StealthSpot._cover_extras`), so the picture is exactly the room's with the player slipped in behind and the top of their head over the back. This is only
possible because planned furniture is separate sprites: no slice shader. The rule table checks a "behind" piece stands out from the wall (the `front` plane — a bookcase against
the wall has no behind), is at least 30 x 36, and is in a living room / bedroom / study / dining room; `stealth_test` shows it refuse a bookcase, a pouf and a toilet.
An armchair is smaller than a crouched person: part of them shows beside it — that is "beside a chair", and in the dark of the hide screen it reads.

**The wall beside a doorway** (`Stealth.WALL_HIDE`, `StealthSpot.add_doorway`, `room._add_doorway_hides`). The owner, on the first capture (a crouched figure straddling the
frame, a prompt on each side): *"it should be more like a standing flat and straight up against the wall on either side, up from the main y plane so as to indicate the player
has moved to a spot out of sight of enemies. Then the contextual left or right side based on where enemies might be coming from. This needs to not be an overwhelming prompt."*
So:

- **One place per doorway** — each doorway between two rooms, and the flat's front door. Not the solid end wall (nothing passes it).
- **Standing**, straight, back to the wall, facing the room, stepped back to where the doorway's opening begins (floor depth 338: 15 px up from the walking line); the
  **door frame's jamb is drawn over them** (`ModuleWalls.draw_door_frame`, the same drawing the walls make). They come away in the stance they went in with.
- **The side is the game's choice** (`StealthSpot.side_for`): the side of the wall **away from the nearest of the dead that are up** — one coming from the right-hand room
  finds nobody, they are flat against the left of the wall, and the press takes them through the doorway to get there. With nobody about, the side they are on.
- **A side is only used if its corner is bare**: 29 px from the wall with nothing standing in it, read off the room's own furniture (a planned room's wall / front / floor
  pieces; a baked room's clear-floor skyline plus what its story laid there). If the side that would hide them is not bare the doorway offers **nothing** — the other side is
  in plain view of what is coming. At the front door there is only the inside: with one of the dead already in the flat it offers nothing.
- **A quiet prompt**: `[E] Hide`, two words, and only when they have all but stopped beside the doorway **and** hiding is on their mind — crouched, or one of the dead up and
  within 420 px (`Stealth.wants_hiding`). Walking through a doorway shows nothing.

**How often** (`tools/hide_census.tscn`, 50 planned rooms a type): a piece to hide in or behind — living room 100 %, bathroom 60 %, bedroom 40 %, study 10 %, **dining room
0 %, kitchen 0 %**; a doorway corner is bare 11-33 % of the time (bedroom 16, living 17, study 28, dining 33, kitchen 21, bathroom 11), so roughly one doorway in three has a
usable side. **That is the honest gap in "clear and present":** kitchens and dining rooms have nothing but a lucky doorway and, at night, the dark. The fix that would make
doorways reliable is a furniture-planner rule — keep one corner beside each doorway free — which re-lays every planned room, so it is a proposal, not something done here.

**Darkness** and the **torch**: `TORCH_AND_DARK.md`.

**Cover, for a fight that does not exist** (owner: *"hide beside a chair, it can be cover to avoid bullets from a neighbour who is shooting at the player, and then the player
if they have a gun can shoot from cover. I'm not saying we need it now, but if we have the tools ready and in place"*): every hiding place states `shield` (the share of a shot
it would stop: a wall 1.0, a cast-iron bath 0.9, a sofa 0.6, an armchair 0.5, a wardrobe door 0.25, a shower curtain 0, the dark 0) and `fire` (could you shoot back from
there: over a sofa, round a door frame, over a bath's rim — yes; from inside a wardrobe or behind a curtain — no). `Stealth.cover_info(player)` and
`Stealth.shot_stopped(player, roll)` read them. **Nothing consumes them**: no neighbour shoots at the player today, and a hidden player cannot attack.

**A bug this found (fixed):** one `[interact]` press could bring you out of a hiding place and put you straight back in — and open the door beside it. The player polls the
press in the physics step, the hiding place and the door poll it in the idle step of the same frame; by then the player was no longer hidden. Headless tests mostly put the two
steps in different frames, which is why round 49q's tests never met it; at 60 fps they share a frame every time. `InteractArbiter.claim()` now spends the press when the player
uses it to come out. `stealth_test` `same_press` runs the two steps back to back in one frame; it fails without the fix.
