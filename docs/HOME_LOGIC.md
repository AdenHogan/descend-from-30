# HOME LOGIC — how a home keeps its things

> GENERATED by `python3 tools/art/home_logic.py --write` from `tools/art/home_logic.py` + `tools/art/furnlib.py` — do not hand-edit (the gate fails when it is stale). To change a rule, change the
> table in `home_logic.py` and regenerate. Owner round 48k: "a logic list of how homes operate… internal intelligence on where things go".

Every rule below is DATA with a check that can fail. **Registration** (`furnlib.piece`) refuses a piece that breaks one; the planner will not deal it (`FurniturePlanner.eligible`);
`FurniturePlanner.validate_state` re-checks every plan; `tests/furniture_test.gd` runs that over thousands of seeds and proves each rule fails on a plan that breaks it.

## 1. What every thing IS, and the rooms a home could keep it in

Every piece is ONE kind of thing (`tools/art/home_kinds.py`). A kind lists the ONLY rooms a home could plausibly keep it in — an upper bound, not a recipe. A piece is registered in some of
those rooms (its `rooms` weights); registration refuses a piece outside the bound, refuses a piece that has no kind, and the planner and `validate_state` re-check it at runtime.

## 2. Keeping: the floor is for things a home keeps on a floor

A piece on the FLOOR plane must carry a `keep` kind — the reason a home keeps it there. A kind may set a smallest height and the only rooms it is kept in.
A piece tagged `small` is never kept on the floor. A thing on a SURFACE is at most **40 px** tall; a plant on a surface at most **26 px**.

## 3a. One of a kind: a room is not doubled up

A room never holds more than this many things of a group, and the same drawing is never hung twice on one wall (a picture's `limit` is 1). Found by looking at planned rooms the way a player does:
a formal dining room could hang a grandfather clock, a cuckoo clock and a wall clock; a houseshare two matching wall clocks side by side.

| group | kinds | most in one room | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| clock | `wall_clock`, `long_case_clock` | 1 | one clock a room: a home does not hang three |
| television | `tv` | 1 | one television a room |
| ceiling light | `ceiling_light`, `chandelier` | 1 | one light in the middle of the ceiling |
| mirror | `mirror` | 1 | one mirror on the wall (a dressing table brings its own) |
| trophy | `hunting` | 1 | one stag head or hunting print |
| hearth | `fireplace` | 1 | one fireplace |
| instrument | `instrument` | 2 | a piano and a guitar at most |
| radiator | `radiator` | 1 | one radiator under the window wall |
| banner | `banner` | 1 | one party banner |

## 3. Belonging: a thing that goes with something is put beside it

A floor piece that declares `near` (shoes → `sleeping`, a bin → `unit`) is only ever placed within **32 px** of a body carrying one of those tags. If the room has none, the piece is
not placed at all — it is never scattered at random. `validate_state` re-checks it.

## 4. Where it may stand (the planner's structural rules, unchanged)

The wall line is exclusive; nothing tall stands in front of the window; everything on the floor lies behind the walking lane (feet ≤ 122) and never in a step-up spot's stand zone; a thing
on top stands ON a surface that has a slot for it (`tools/art/support_audit.py`); hung things hang clear of every piece; a desk wants its chair; a piece's `limit`; at least `min_front`
nodes reachable from the walking lane. See the header of `scripts/furniture_planner.gd`.

## 5. The same rule in the baked story rooms

`scripts/room_growth.gd` places houseplants in the baked modules. A flat's floor takes only the TALL pots (`FLOOR_POTS` / `FLOOR_DEAD_POTS`, each at least 48 px — the plant rule above); small pots stand on a
balcony, where pots really do stand on the floor. `tests/growth_test.gd` checks every floor pot against this table.
